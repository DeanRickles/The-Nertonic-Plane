# City of Jerisor
Based in [[The Fern Region (North)]].

The city of Jerisor, is more of a religious order. Only the teachings and understanding of God. Those who do not see the ways of the one true God are seen as heretics.

## Military 
Each level has their own military that is marked by badge with diamonds that supersedes the others, more diamonds the higher rank. One large diamond is the true authority mounted in the staff of Royalty.


Order of authority:
- Single mark
- Double mark
- Triple mark
- Banner

Marker representative:
- Upper (Red Rose) 
- Middle (White rose)
- Lower (Black Rose)


## Temple of Mornin 
Every Saturday afternoon everyone goes to Temple service to God, Mornin.  Where the  light is at it’s peak and shines down the venter of the mountain lighting all three layers at once.

## Infrastructure
> [!Note] Use the rule of 3, everything will have three.

Jerisor is built top of the mountain with wide long walls surrounding the city and its inhabitants.

The city is built up into three floors built inside the mountains caverns.
* Upper | Middle | Lower

There is one centre column that runs from the top to bottom of the mountain. On the upper and middle layers, a large crystal dividing the floors but allowing some light through. On the lower floor a old large tree sits.

During the peak of the day the crystals beams the light, glowing to their fullest creating true daylight on the middle and lower layers.

Travel between the layers is done around the core.

#### Description 
Managed by the Royalty.

Mainly partying, politics, and academics.

(Basically style as a university.)

#### Infrastructure
Is three key spires. In the city centre there for the king and the Queen the East one for the son and the their mage consorts.

# Template City checklist

### **Make it full of potential by taking your time**

A city is nothing if it doesn't have lots of nooks and crannies to discover and love, especially if just walking into a shop or witnessing an event can ignite an entire campaign arc. You're going to want to spend a lot of time crafting this city before your players even set foot in it. Much of what we do as DMs can be hand waved and figured out as we go along / patched up after the fact. A city is really something that you're going to want to etch into stone long before the dice hit the table.

Also, as a side note, leave it open ended for maximum fun. There's a lot going on but none of it has a resolution. There's a gang shaking down the alchemy shop. A baroness is getting a little greedy when it comes to games of power. An expedition is setting out to explore the lumber mill abandoned years ago under mysterious circumstances. As a DM, have nothing planned for any of these until the party commits to pursuing one. Then you can sprinkle in back story elements, craft an adventure you're excited about running, and everyone will have a great time.

### **The souls of the city**

These interesting locations are nothing without interesting NPCs! I would flesh out the NPCs at all these places with a one paragraph description.

>[!example]- Example NPC blurp
>Herald runs the magical item store. He is extremely fair with his prices but absolutely cannot stand haggling. He has a mid western US accent and is a family man who pretends to be religious but secretly couldn't care less. He puts this act on to please his wife, whom he is deeply in love with.

Just this blurp alone can let you riff hours of dialogue should you need to. It makes them a real character that the party will identify with. They will not all be hits, but about half a dozen will, and they will cherish these NPCs for as long as the game lasts.

The formula to success is a distinctive manner of speaking / voice, a clear and easily communicated personality, and a reason to be interacting with the party.

### **Secrets lurk in the dark**

Another great tip is to bury a bunch of secrets within the city. There's a lot of people who live here, and some of them are up to something. Good, bad, who knows, but hide a lot of juicy stuff for the players to find. This requires a lot of patience; I sat on one particular revelation for the entire campaign that was only revealed at the very last session. Bury them at a variety of length; some will be revealed without much digging, but others should be long cons. It gives the city a feeling of being alive and dynamic, always changing as players see it in a new light constantly.

The nature of the secrets should be about the city itself and ideally relate to the players. Small stuff would be something the local cheap tavern is watering down their beer, large things would be like the previous king, beloved by the public, did not die in his sleep but was rather murdered because he was actually a cultist of orcus! You get the idea, just make it dramatic and interesting!

### **Politics**

The world should feel that the power struggle would move on without the players. Here are some example of what the platers could see or be involved in.

> [!INFO]- Politics (example, all of it between two or three powerful people)
> - [ ] Power struggles
> - [ ] turf wars
> - [ ] wrath
> - [ ] danger
> - [ ] necessity
> - [ ] revenge

Put this place under pressure and let the players pick a side then have the other side screw them over. Nothing is better than finally crushing a powerful enemy that has bothered you for far too long.

### **A saint for the sinners**

A patron that is interested in shepherding and sponsoring the party is a ton of fun to add in. They may hear of the party and contact them by mail, eager to gently implore the budding adventuring party in certain directions via the age of the mechanism of coin. The patron in our game was a manipulative scheming egomaniac who didn't exactly have the parties best interest at heart, but was far from overtly hostile and often quite helpful. This spawned a very tense and fantastically fun razors edge game of cat and mouse between them that was still some of the most fun I've ever had in DnD.

### **"Side show" NPCs**

Last tidbit - add some NPCs who are there just to be interesting. They don't have to be there for any useful purpose, just there to be characters that draw the eye. A local tavern bard who is known for his escapades all around the city. A notorious drunk who is always... always.... causing trouble. If you've ever played Skyrim, the raving preacher in Whiterun is a great example. I can activate your neuron right now by typing WRITHING IN THE FILTH AND MUD.

These guys are great as one time quest givers, sources of useful information about the city, or just fun to have around. Get zanny with these and you'll never be sorry you have them up your sleeve to throw into a situation that may need a dash of chaos.
